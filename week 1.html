Land Lines

—Zach Liberman

1) What is this work

This is an exploration of Google Earth's satellite imagery dataset.

It combines coding technology, big data, interaction with user and creative perspectives.

Zach and the team pre-analyzed many satellite images using algorithm and java to detect lines from the images.

Users are encouraged to interact by drawing and dragging mouse to create lines, and the project responds in real-time by matching the lines to the most similar features in the lanscape pictures.

2) What I find interesting about it

1. Interaction Design Part

Zach uses a really creative way to bring up users's interest. Things like satellite photos could easily feel static and unremarkable, but Zach turns them into something people can actively engage with and creates a sense of participation that makes people want to learn more about the landscapes.

Moreover, the gesture based drawing mirrors exactly the most outstanding part of the images, leading people to notice the figures of landscapes that they might not paid attention to before. This made me realize that sometimes tchnology or coding isn't really the point. What matters most is to be creative and interact with people. Coding is just a media or tool.

2. Story Behind the Work

It moves me a lot when I read about the process behind this project. Zach and the team went through multiple trials and errors to achieve the final result.

I also face a lot of problems while doing my own work, and often feel overwhelmed, depressed and sometimes doubt about myself. But reading about his experience let me understand that I'm not alone. Even someone this skilled and professional still runs into difficulties and has to keep doing experiments, so it's also pretty normal for me to struggle with problems.
